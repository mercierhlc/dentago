## What Went Wrong & How to Fix It

### 1. Came unprepared — hadn't tested the product before the demo
**What happened:** Jumped into a live demo without running through the product beforehand. Any bugs, broken flows, or confusion mid-demo immediately kills trust.

**Fix going forward:**
- Do a full solo walkthrough of the demo flow 15–30 mins before every call
- Have a specific demo account pre-loaded with realistic product searches ready to go
- If anything looks broken, have a fallback (screenshots, Loom recording) rather than winging it live

---

### 2. Left it way too last minute
**What happened:** No prep time, reactive rather than proactive going into the call.

**Fix going forward:**
- Block 30 mins before every Calendly booking as a non-negotiable prep window
- The night before: review their form answers, prep 3 tailored talking points based on their suppliers/spend/role
- Morning of: re-read the form, run through the demo, confirm the Meet link works

---

### 3. Unplanned and unscripted
**What happened:** No clear structure to the call — likely jumped around, missed key points, or didn't land the pitch cleanly.

**Fix going forward:**
- Follow the standard call structure every time:
  1. Quick intro + set agenda (1 min)
  2. Discovery questions based on their form (5–7 min)
  3. Pitch tailored to what they said (5 min)
  4. Demo (5 min)
  5. Handle objections
  6. Close / next steps (2 min)
- Keep a printed or pinned script visible during calls as a reference
- Never freestyle the close — have exact words ready

---

### 4. Stuttered a lot
**What happened:** Nerves/lack of prep led to verbal stumbles which reduces perceived authority and confidence.

**Fix going forward:**
- Prep reduces stuttering more than anything — know the material cold
- Do a 2-minute spoken warmup before calls (say your intro out loud)
- Slow down deliberately — most stuttering comes from speaking too fast
- It's okay to pause. Silence reads as confidence, not weakness

---

### 5. Skipped discovery entirely — jumped straight into screen share
**Timestamp:** 0:19
**What happened:** Karuna had just started talking about her suppliers (multiple companies — Henry Schein for most, Orthocare for ortho, B&C for cleaning) and you immediately cut to "I'm gonna go ahead and screen share." You missed the entire discovery phase. You never asked her spend, pain points, how she compares prices, what frustrates her about ordering — all of which would have made the demo land 10x harder.

**Fix going forward:**
- Never screen share before you've done at least 5 minutes of discovery
- Let them talk. The more they describe their pain, the more the demo feels like a solution to *their* problem, not a generic product pitch
- Use their exact words during the demo: *"You said you're using Henry Schein for most things — let me show you exactly what that looks like"*

---

### 6. Demo was disorganised and narrated badly
**Timestamp:** 0:19 – 4:02
**What happened:** The screen share started with confusion ("it wasn't, I'm not sure what happened there"), then rambled through the product without a clear flow. You explained the process verbally while fumbling through the UI. You also went off-topic talking about almost doing dentistry in A-levels — totally irrelevant, wastes time and breaks momentum.

**Fix going forward:**
- Have one rehearsed demo narrative: "Here's the problem → here's what Dentago does → let me show you"
- Don't narrate confusion. If something doesn't load, move on calmly
- Cut all personal tangents during the demo — save rapport-building for the opener and close
- The demo path should be: Search → Compare prices → Add to cart → Savings shown → Place order → Invoice

---

### 7. Over-explained the savings calculation
**Timestamp:** 4:09
**What happened:** Spent 4+ minutes explaining *how* the savings are calculated (most expensive minus least expensive), which is the least interesting part. Clients don't care about the maths — they care about the number. "You'll save £77 on this order, £4k a year" is all you need.

**Fix going forward:**
- Show the saving, say the number, move on
- One sentence max: *"It compares your negotiated prices across all suppliers and shows you the cheapest"*
- Let the UI do the explaining — your job is to highlight the emotional impact of the number

---

### 8. Weak response to "does it have access to all suppliers?"
**Timestamp:** 8:25
**What happened:** Karuna asked a direct question about supplier coverage. Your answer mentioned "15 suppliers" but then got tangled — "on the one I've run locally it's going to be a lot more than that" which sounds confused and undermines confidence. You also said "I guess it's not loading" mid-demo which is a red flag.

**Fix going forward:**
- Prep a clean answer: *"We have 15 UK suppliers live including Henry Schein, Dental Sky, Kent Express, Dental Directory and more. We're adding new suppliers every month."*
- Never say "I guess it's not loading" — have a fallback ready or just move on smoothly

### 9. Unverified claims — £800k GMV, "a month and a half in"
**Timestamp:** 11:31
**What happened:** You told Karuna you're doing £800,000 in GMV and you're only a month and a half in. This is a big claim. If she ever cross-references or asks for proof and you can't back it up, it destroys trust completely.

**Fix going forward:**
- Only use numbers you can back up
- If you want social proof, use the clients you actually have and real savings figures
- A more credible version: *"We're processing orders across multiple practices and the savings we're seeing are consistently 8–15%"*

---

### 10. Revealed 3% commission model unprompted and in detail
**Timestamp:** 12:17 – 12:44
**What happened:** You went into detail about how you take 3% from suppliers, how suppliers approach you to buy data, how you're operating at 90% margins, and how you could take £20k/month from Henry Schein. This is oversharing. It raises questions: *"If you're making this much, why do you need our data?"* It also makes you sound more like a supplier-side business than a clinic-side one.

**Fix going forward:**
- Business model answer should be one sentence: *"We take a small margin from suppliers — you never pay anything"*
- Never discuss your margins, profitability, or supplier data offers on a sales call
- Keep the framing: **"We're on your side, not the suppliers'"**

---

### 11. Close was weak and uncertain
**Timestamp:** 15:00 – 15:56
**What happened:** The close was *"do you want to do that together? Or I can leave you guys to it... it's like, down to you."* That's a non-close. You handed the decision back to them and made it easy to say "we'll think about it." The fact that they said yes anyway is testament to the value prop — not the close.

**Fix going forward:**
- Own the close. Don't give an opt-out: *"Let's get you set up now — it takes 10 minutes and I can approve your docs straight away while we're on the call"*
- Remove "or I can leave you to it" — that's an escape hatch you're handing them for free
- Always book the next specific step before hanging up
